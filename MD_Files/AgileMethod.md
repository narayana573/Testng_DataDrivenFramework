### **Agile & Scrum Essentials**

* **Epic:** A large body of work that can be broken down into smaller, manageable pieces called **User Stories**. Epics usually take multiple sprints to complete.
* **User Story:** An informal, general explanation of a software feature written from the perspective of the end-user.
* *Format:* "As a [user], I want [action] so that [value/benefit]."


* **Product Backlog:** The master list for the entire product, owned by the Product Owner, containing all potential work.
* **Sprint Backlog:** A subset of Product Backlog items selected by the development team for a specific, short iteration (sprint) to complete during that time.
* **Sprint / Iteration:** A time-boxed period (usually 2–4 weeks) during which a specific set of work is completed and made ready for review.
* **Story Point:** A unit of measure used in Agile to estimate the overall effort required to fully implement a product backlog item. It accounts for **complexity, amount of work, and risk/uncertainty.**
* **Spillover:** Spillover	Work that was started in one Sprint but not finished, "spilling over" into the next.

### **Scrum Ceremonies**

* **Scrum Meeting (Daily Stand-up):** A 15-minute daily meeting for the team to synchronize. Each member answers:
1. What did I do yesterday?
2. What will I do today?
3. Are there any blockers (impediments)?


* **Sprint Retrospective:** Held at the end of a sprint. The team reflects on the past sprint to identify what went well, what didn't, and how to improve the process for the next sprint.

### **QA Specific Interview Tips**

| Concept | Key QA Takeaway |
| --- | --- |
| **Acceptance Criteria** | Always mention that you use the Acceptance Criteria in a User Story to derive your **Test Cases**. |
| **Definition of Done (DoD)** | For a QA, "Done" usually means all test cases are executed, all critical bugs are closed, and regression testing is complete. |
| **Backlog Grooming** | This is where QA provides input on the "testability" of stories and helps refine estimates. |

### **1. Leadership & Team Management**

* **"Can you describe a time when a team member was not meeting quality standards? How did you handle it?"**
* *Focus:* Mentorship, setting clear expectations, and professional communication.


* **"How do you prioritize testing tasks when your team is short-staffed or under a tight deadline?"**
* *Focus:* Risk-based testing. Mention how you identify "critical path" items that must be tested first.


* **"Tell me about a time you had to resolve a conflict between a developer and a tester regarding a bug's severity."**
* *Focus:* Data-driven decision-making. Use the requirements/user stories as the source of truth to justify the bug's impact.



### **2. Stakeholder & UAT Management**

* **"How do you explain technical testing risks to non-technical stakeholders or business users?"**
* *Focus:* Translating "bugs" into "business impact" (e.g., "This issue could prevent 20% of users from completing a purchase").


* **"Describe a situation where the business users (UAT) found a critical bug that the QA team missed. How did you manage the situation?"**
* *Focus:* Root cause analysis (RCA) and process improvement rather than placing blame.


* **"How do you ensure that UAT testers (who are often busy business users) stay engaged and complete their testing on time?"**
* *Focus:* Preparation, clear test scripts, and providing daily progress dashboards.



---

### **3. Process & Strategy**

* **"If you are joined a project with no existing testing process, what are the first three things you would implement?"**
* *Focus:* 1. Understanding requirements, 2. Establishing a Bug Life Cycle, 3. Creating a Test Plan/Strategy.


* **"How do you decide which test cases to automate and which to keep manual?"**
* *Focus:* ROI of automation. Mention that repetitive, high-volume regression tests are best for automation, while UX and complex new features are better for manual/exploratory testing.



---

### **4. Handling Challenges**

* **"Tell me about a time a project was going to miss its release date due to testing delays. What did you do?"**
* *Focus:* Transparency. Mention providing a "Risk Report" to leadership and suggesting a phased release or "descoping" non-critical features.


* **"Describe your experience managing testing for a complex integration project. How did you ensure end-to-end quality?"**
* *Focus:* Collaboration with other teams and the importance of a clear "Integration Test Plan."



### **How to Structure Your Answers: The STAR Method**

For every behavioral question, follow this structure to keep your answer concise:

1. **S**ituation: Set the scene (briefly).
2. **T**ask: What was the specific challenge?
3. **A**ction: What **you** specifically did (the "Lead" actions).
4. **R**esult: The positive outcome (e.g., "We released on time with zero critical bugs").
